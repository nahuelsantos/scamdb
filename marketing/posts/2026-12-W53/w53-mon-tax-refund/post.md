## Instagram (5 slides)

```ig
“You’re eligible for a tax refund of $1,284.50. Verify your bank account within 48 hours.”

Good news with a deadline and a link is a classic. The page behind it asks for your Social Security number and bank details, and that’s what they wanted all along.

Check refunds yourself: type the IRS address into your browser and sign in to your account. Same for Social Security statements.

Got one like this, scam or genuine? Send it to us at scamdb.dev/report (link in bio). Reports stay private.

#scamalert #taxscam #irs #scamawareness
```

**Alt text**

1. Hand-drawn illustration. Scam of the week · US · text, email. Headline: ““You’re owed a refund””. Tax season starts soon, and fake refunds start before it.
2. An example written by scamdb, from “IRS Notice”: “IRS Notice: You are eligible for a tax refund of $1,284.50. Your direct deposit information is missing. Verify your bank account within 48 hours to receive your refund: [link]”. Warning signs: Money you weren’t expecting; A deadline; A link that wants your bank details.
3. How it goes, in four hand-drawn steps (see the catalogue entry).
4. What to do: Don’t click, scan or open. Delete it. Check refunds yourself: type the IRS address and sign in. The IRS doesn’t text or email you asking for bank details. Entered bank details? Call your bank straight away.
5. scamdb logo. Got one like this? Send it to scamdb.dev/report. Scam or genuine, both help. Reports stay private.
