# Mon Nov 30 (Cyber Monday) · Scam of the week: the fake fraud alert

- **Pillar:** Scam of the week
- **Catalogue entry:** https://scamdb.dev/catalogue/us/bank-fraud-alert-zelle/
- **Real message:** from FBI IC3 PSA I-041422-PSA, "Cybercriminals Trick Victims into Transferring Funds to 'Reverse'
  Instant Payments", Apr 14, 2022: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2022/PSA220414. The FBI shows the texts "similar to the
  following": "Free Msg- (Insert financial institution name here) Bank Fraud Alert- Did You Attempt an Instant Payment

## Instagram (carousel, 5 slides)

```ig
It starts with a text that looks like your bank: “Did you attempt a payment of $5,000? Reply YES or NO.”

You reply NO, because you didn’t. Then a “fraud specialist” calls. They sound helpful and they know your name. To “protect” your money, they ask for the code your bank just texted you, or walk you through sending money to a “safe” account with Zelle or a transfer. That account is theirs.

Swipe for the real text (as published by the FBI), the script and what to do.

The one thing to remember: your bank will never ask you to move money to protect it.

Paste a message on scamdb.dev (link in bio) to check it. Share this with someone who banks on their phone.

#scamalert #bankfraud #fraudprevention #scamawareness #zelle
```

**Alt text**

1. Hand-drawn bank building with a pink heart. Headline: “The fake fraud alert”. It starts with a text from “your
   bank”. It ends with you moving your own money to them.
2. A real scam text, as published by the FBI: “Free Msg- ([bank name]) Bank Fraud Alert- Did You Attempt an Instant
   Payment in the amount of $5,000.00? REPLY YES or NO or 1 To STOP ALERTS”. After a “NO”: “Our fraud specialist will
   be contacting you shortly”. Three signs: looks like your bank, a payment you didn’t make, then a call from “fraud”.
3. How it goes: a text or call says there is a suspicious charge; a fake fraud department says your money is at risk;
   they ask you to read out the code your bank just texted you; they walk you through sending money to a “safe”
   account.
4. What to do: your bank will never ask you to move money to protect it; never read out a code; hang up and call the
   number on the back of your card; if you sent money, tell your bank right away.
5. Got a message like this? Paste it on scamdb.dev.
