# Tue Nov 24 · How the trick works: the small amount (fake toll text)

- **Pillar:** How the trick works
- **Catalogue entry:** https://scamdb.dev/catalogue/us/toll-text/
- **Real message:** "(State Toll Service Name): We've noticed an outstanding toll amount of $12.51 on your record. To
  avoid a late fee of $50.00, visit [link] to settle your balance." From FBI IC3 PSA I-041224-PSA, "Smishing Scam
  Regarding Debt for Road Toll Services", Apr 12, 2024: https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240412. The FBI wrote
  "(State Toll Service Name)" in place of the real toll program, and says complaints used "almost identical language".
  The link is replaced with [link]; never post the original domain.
- **Facts from:** FTC, "New trends in reports of imposter scams", May 7, 2026 (in the catalogue entry).
- **Images:** `01-ig.png`, `02-ig.png` (Instagram, 2 slides), `03-x.png` (X)

## Instagram (2 slides)

```ig
Why do fake toll texts ask for $12.51 and not $500?

Because a small bill feels too small to question. You think “I probably did drive through a toll”, tap the link and pay. The scammers don’t want the $12.51. They want the card number you just typed in.

This is a real one, as published by the FBI. Notice the four signs: a toll program you know, a small amount, a threat on top ($50 late fee) and a link to pay.

If you get one: don’t tap. Go to your toll program’s website by typing it yourself, or call the number on your toll account. If you already paid, call your card issuer.

Paste any text on scamdb.dev (link in bio) to see if it matches a known scam.

#scamalert #textscam #fraudprevention #scamawareness
```
