# scamdb brand profile (social media)

Drawn from `docs/marketing.md`, `docs/vision.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `docs/social.md` (2026-11-18). If those docs change,
they win; update this file to match.

## In one line

**Scammers change numbers and links every week, but the script stays the same for years. Learn the script and you'll
spot the scam.**

## What we are

A free, open field guide to scams: 1,199 documented scams from 76 countries. Each one has its script step by step, the
warning signs, what to do and who to report it to. Anyone can paste a message on scamdb.dev and see if it matches a
known scam. The check runs in the browser and nothing is sent. Developers get the same check as a free API, and the
whole catalogue as open data (CC BY 4.0).

## What the accounts are for

1. **Main goal:** people check a message on scamdb.dev, for free, and think of scamdb next time a message looks wrong.
2. People report scams they get (scamdb.dev/report).
3. Developers try the API (scamdb.dev/docs).

We don't count followers as the goal. We count link taps and shares ("send this to your mum").

## Who we talk to

| Who | What they need from us | What they do with it |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyday people** who just got a strange text, call or WhatsApp | A quick "is this one of those?" answer and one thing to do | Check the message, ignore it, warn family |
| **The family helper**: the adult child or grandchild who gets "is this real?" from relatives | Something clear and kind to forward | Share and save posts, send to family groups |
| **Older adults** (grandparent, fake bank and fake government scams hit them hardest) | Big text, plain words, no tech talk, no shame | Read, ask family, call the real bank |
| **People who help others**: journalists, teachers, librarians, senior and consumer groups, police prevention teams | Accurate, sourced, dated content they can repost | Repost, quote, link to entries |
| **Developers and companies** (banks, telcos, messaging apps) | Proof the data is real and open, and an API that works | Visit the docs, try the API |

**Notes on the audience**

- On **Instagram**, the family helper is the most likely sharer. Carousels get saved and sent in DMs, so every carousel
  should work as a "send this to someone" post.
- **X** suits people who help others: journalists, researchers, police and consumer-agency accounts, and developers.
  Speed matters there: when an official warning lands, answer it the same day ("Trending now").
- Older adults use Facebook and WhatsApp far more than X. We're not on Facebook, so on Instagram we reach them mostly
  through their families. That's why posts say "share this with someone who'd get this message".
- Assume US readers unless a post says otherwise. Dollar amounts, USPS, IRS, Zelle and the FTC are familiar to them.

## Voice

**A calm friend who knows the tricks.**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "They send a text that looks like USPS." | "Beware!!! New USPS scam!!!" |
| "It happens to careful people too." | "Don't be fooled." / "Only fools fall for this." |
| "Here's the one thing to do: hang up and call the number on your card." | Long lists of 12 tips |
| "scamdb matches known scam patterns, so it can miss brand-new ones." | "Never get scammed again." |
| "The FTC warns…" (with the date) | Unsourced claims or numbers |

- Short sentences. Plain words. No jargon ("smishing" only when explained: "a scam text, or 'smishing'").
- "They" did it (the scammers), never "you fell for it".
- No ALL CAPS, no siren or warning emoji, no fear-bait hooks ("You could lose everything").
- Be honest about limits, every time we promote the check.

## Hard rules (never break)

1. **Only real, documented scams.** A real message is shown only when a public, dated source quotes it, and the source
   is named on the image and in the caption. Anything we wrote is labelled "An example of how this one reads" or
   "written by scamdb".
2. **No impersonation.** No real logos, brand colours or look-alike screens. Names appear in text only (for example,
   quoting a warning, or "USPS" inside a quoted scam text).
3. **No personal data.** Use the placeholders [number], [link], [name] and [bank]. Never post a live scam link.
4. **Reports are private.** Nothing from scamdb.dev/report ever appears in a post.
5. **No paid ads, and the owner publishes.** Drafts only. No scheduling or publishing tools unless the owner asks.

## Look

Every image is drawn with the site's own helpers, so it looks like scamdb.dev:

- Paper `#eceee4`, deep green ink `#1d2a20`, card `#fafbf6`, **one pink accent** `#d63d74` per image, used for the
  thing to notice (the numbered red flags, the SCAM stamp, or a pink detail in a drawing). Lime highlighter behind one
  key phrase.
- Space Grotesk for headlines, IBM Plex Sans for text, IBM Plex Mono for labels and sources, Patrick Hand for the
  handwritten notes.
- Pen-and-shading drawings from `lib/sketch.js` and the catalogue icons. No photos, stock icons, emoji or gradients.
- Every image carries the scamdb logo and scamdb.dev in the footer, and a source line when it shows a message.
- Sizes: Instagram 1080×1350 (portrait), X 1600×900.

To remake or change an image, see `marketing/README.md`.

## Hashtags

- Instagram: 3–5 per post, at the end of the caption. Core set: `#scamalert` `#scamawareness` `#fraudprevention`
  `#onlinesafety` `#phishing`, plus one topical tag (`#textscam`, `#elderfraud`, `#blackfriday`).
- X: 0–1 per post. Hashtags don't help much on X, and they use up characters.
