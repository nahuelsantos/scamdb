# scamdb marketing playbook (Phase 1)

Built from `foundation.md` (Stages 1–3). It covers what to post, how, when, and how to tell if it's working.
`brand-profile.md` has the voice and rules; `calendar.md` has the dated posts.

## 1. The one goal for Phase 1

**Get messages from other people.** Scam messages above all, and genuine ones too. Everything else (followers, likes,
views) only matters if it leads there.

**North-star number:** reports per week that weren't sent by the owner.
**Phase 2 starts when:** 300 real scam reports + 50 genuine ones from other people are in, **or** after 12 weeks,
whichever comes first. At that point we review and decide. (Owner to confirm the numbers.)

## 2. Who we talk to, and where

| | Instagram (@scamdb.dev) | X (@scamdb) |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 audience | The family helper, 25–45 | The same people, plus journalists and consumer/police accounts |
| What they get | Breakdowns they can send to family | The same, in 280 characters, fast |
| The ask | "Send it to scamdb.dev/report" | Same, in a reply to our own post |
| Not yet | Developer content | Developer content (comes in Phase 2) |

Messages are welcome from anywhere, in any language. Posts are in English, with US examples first.

## 3. Tone in one line

**Dry wit, calm, never at the victim's expense.** "Because nobody argues about twelve dollars." Not: "Don't be
stupid." Not: "WARNING!!!"

## 4. Post templates

Every template ends with the same ask. Fill in the brackets.

### A. Scam of the week (Monday · Instagram carousel, X image + reply)

1. **Cover:** the scam's best-known line as a headline + one-line hook. *"“Your package can't be delivered.” The
   busiest scam text of the holidays."*
2. **The message:** the real text (with its source) or a labelled written example, with 2–4 numbered red flags.
3. **The script:** the catalogue entry's 4 steps, with their icons.
4. **What to do:** 3–4 checkboxes. The first is the one thing to remember.
5. **The ask:** "Got one like this? Send it: scamdb.dev/report".

Caption pattern: hook (1 line) · what happens (2–3 lines) · "Swipe for…" · the one thing to remember · the ask ·
"Share this with [who'd get it]" · 3–5 hashtags.

### B. How the trick works (Tuesday · 1–2 images)

Headline = the trick as a question or a rule (*"Why only $12.51?"*, *"Nobody real asks for gift cards"*). Image 2 =
the message with flags. Caption: why the trick works on smart people (2–3 lines) → what to do → the ask.

### C. Real or scam? (Wednesday · Instagram 2 slides, X image + answer in a reply)

Slide 1: two messages, A and B, with a note on what's real and what's written. Caption: "Guess in the comments, then
swipe." Slide 2: the answer stamp + 2–3 reasons + the honest part (*"from the text alone you can't always tell"*).
**Best pair:** a real, credited scam next to the owner's own genuine message (company names as placeholders).

### D. Around the world (Thursday · 1–2 images)

One script, shown in 3–5 countries, using the words from each catalogue entry (labelled when written by us). Caption
ends with: "Got one like this, anywhere in the world, in any language? Send it: scamdb.dev/report".

### E. Behind scamdb (Friday · 1–2 images)

How the check works, what it can't do, new countries, numbers (when we have them). This is the most direct place to
ask for genuine messages: *"the real ones teach the check not to raise false alarms."*

### F. Trending now (any day, replaces the planned post)

When the FTC, FBI, police or a bank publishes a warning that matches a catalogue entry: one image (the warning's quoted
message if there is one, with source) + the link to the warning + the catalogue link. Same day. Speed beats polish.

## 5. Video templates (Reels on Instagram, video on X)

All vertical 1080×1920, 25–30 s, captions always on, drawn in the site style, your voice (or captions only, see the
test below).

| Format | Structure (seconds) | How often |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Teardown** | 0–3 hook question · 3–10 the real message types out (source on screen) · 10–17 the flags light up · 17–23 the point in one line · 23–28 the ask | 1 a week |
| **(b) Real or scam? quiz** | 0–2 "3 seconds" · 2–8 A and B + countdown · 8–13 the stamp · 13–20 why · 20–26 the ask | 1 a week |
| **(d) Screen recording** | 0–3 hook · 3–9 paste on scamdb.dev · 9–16 the real answer · 16–23 the script and what to do · 23–30 the ask | Once, pinned. Remake when the site changes |

**Production rhythm:** one recording session a month, 4–6 scripts at a time (about 20–30 minutes). The owner records;
I animate, mix and caption.

**Posting a Reel:** post it on the same day as the post on the same topic. On Instagram, also share it to your Story
and add it to a Highlight ("How it works", "Scams", "Real or scam?"). Music: add one from Instagram's library at
10–20% volume, or none. On X, post the voice-only version.

## 6. The daily and weekly routine (3 hours a week)

| When | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Read the week's drafts in `marketing/posts/`, ask for changes | 30 min |
| Mon–Fri | Post on Instagram and X (copy, alt text, images are ready); post the X reply with the ask | 10 min/day |
| Mon–Fri | Answer comments and replies: every one in the first hour if you can | 10 min/day |
| Once a week | 15 min of useful replies to others: consumer-protection accounts, journalists covering scams, and (for later) bot-builder communities. Help, don't promote | 15 min |
| Monthly | One recording session | 30 min |

**Rules for replies:**
- Never ask people to paste scam texts in public comments (they might include their number or a real link). Point
  them to scamdb.dev/report.
- If someone lost money: be kind, give the one thing to do (call the bank; report at reportfraud.ftc.gov in the US),
  never blame them.
- If someone says "scamdb got it wrong": thank them, ask them to send it via /report, and don't argue.
- Never confirm or deny anything about a specific person's report in public.

**Questions the test answers:**
1. Which format brings reports: carousels, single images or videos?
2. Voice or captions only? Post the four videos with your voice. If one does much worse on watch time, try a
   captions-only version of the next one and compare.
3. Which topic travels: delivery, toll, bank, gift cards, family?

**What to write down after each post** (a simple note or spreadsheet; 2 minutes a day):

| Number | Where to find it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Reports that day (not yours) | Ask me to count, or `npm run reports list` | **The goal** |
| Link taps | Instagram Insights → the post → "Profile activity / link taps"; X: post analytics (bar-chart icon) → link clicks | Interest in acting |
| Shares + sends | Instagram Insights → shares | The family helper doing their job |
| Saves | Instagram Insights → saves | Useful enough to keep |

Ignore likes and follower counts for now.

**Review on Sun Dec 6** (30 min together): keep what brought reports, drop the bottom third, and plan the next two
weeks (the holiday campaign: deliveries and gifts, Christmas, Boxing Day for the UK, Canada, Australia and New
Zealand).

## 8. Things we never do

- Invent a message and call it real. Show a live scam link. Show anyone's personal data.
- Use a real company's logo or the look of its app.
- Post anything from someone else's report.
- Shame victims, scare people for clicks, or promise scamdb catches everything.
- Pay for ads or schedule posts with tools, unless the owner decides otherwise.
