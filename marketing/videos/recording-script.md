# Recording script: the first 4 videos

One sitting, about 20 minutes. Read everything below from top to bottom.

## Before you press record (5 minutes)

1. **Room:** the quietest, softest room you have. A closet full of clothes or a bedroom with curtains and a bed is
   best. Avoid kitchens, bathrooms and empty rooms (echo).
2. **Silence:** turn off fans and air conditioning, close the window, and put the phone in **airplane mode** after
   opening the recorder.
3. **App:** iPhone → *Voice Memos*. First go to Settings → Apps → Voice Memos → *Audio Quality* → **Lossless**.
4. **Phone position:** the bottom of the phone (the microphone) pointing at your mouth, **a hand's width away**
   (15–20 cm), slightly to the side. Keep that distance the whole time.
5. **Water** nearby. Sit up straight.

## How to read

- **Slowly.** Slower than feels natural. Leave a clear pause at every full stop, and a **2-second pause** between
  lines.
- **Like telling a friend,** with a small smile on the first and last line. Calm, a bit amused at the scammers.
- **Your accent is fine.** Don't put on an American accent. Clear beats perfect.
- **Mistake?** Don't stop. Pause 2 seconds, then read the **whole line** again. I'll cut the bad one.
- Say the **video number** before each video ("Video one"), so I can find them.

## Pronunciation

| Write | Say |
|---|---|
| scamdb | "scam D B" |
| scamdb dot dev | "scam D B dot dev" |
| $12.51 | "twelve dollars and fifty-one cents" (first time), then "twelve fifty-one" |
| Zelle | "zel" (not used aloud in these scripts) |

## The take

1. Press record. Stay silent for **10 seconds** (I use it to clean background noise).
2. Read all four videos once.
3. Without stopping, read all four **a second time**.
4. Stop. Listen back with headphones. If you hear echo, a hum or rubbing, change rooms and do it again.

---

### Video one: "How it works"

> Got a text that feels off? Don't ask the group chat.
>
> Paste it into scam D B dot dev.
>
> It checks it against about twelve hundred known scam scripts. In your browser. Nothing gets sent.
>
> You get the script they're running, and the one thing to do next.
>
> It only knows scams someone has documented. Found a new one? Send it to us.

### Video two: "Why only twelve fifty-one?"

> Why does a fake toll text ask for twelve dollars and fifty-one cents?
>
> Because nobody argues about twelve dollars.
>
> A toll program you know. A small amount. A fifty-dollar threat. And a link.
>
> They don't want the twelve fifty-one. They want the card number you just typed in.
>
> Don't tap. Send it to us instead.

### Video three: "Real or scam?"

> Two texts from your bank. One's a scam. Three seconds.
>
> *(pause: stay silent for 4 seconds)*
>
> B. But look how alike they are. Same bank. Same "if this wasn't you".
>
> So don't reply, and don't call the number in the text. Call the one on your card.
>
> Got either kind? Send it. The real ones teach the check too.

### Video four: "Shopped today?"

> Spent money today? Scammers are counting on it.
>
> A fraud alert asks if you just paid five thousand dollars. You didn't. You reply no.
>
> Then a helpful "fraud specialist" calls, and asks for a code. Or to move your money somewhere safe.
>
> Your bank never asks you to move money to protect it. Ever.
>
> Hang up, call the number on your card, and send the text to us.
